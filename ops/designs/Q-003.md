# Q-003 · Split the price ladder by position

Drafted by the nightly improvement pass, 2026-11-04. Nothing here is built until the item's Decision says approved.

## Problem
The ladder (`warroom/build.py` `ladder`) turns past claims into win chances, but only splits them by estimate size ($10+ vs
under). Overall it is calibrated (leave one waiver run out, 165 claims: 55% won at the 50% price, 82% at the 80% price), but
not by position (health.json `sections.warroom.details.prices.by`, Nov 3):

| Split | n | won at 50% price | won at 80% price | raw estimate miss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RB | 30 | 0.37 | 0.67 | $20.4 |
| WR | 54 | 0.48 | 0.76 | $8.6 |
| QB | 21 | 0.67 | 0.90 | $11.3 |
| TE | 22 | 0.55 | 0.86 | $6.2 |
| DEF | 22 | 0.55 | 0.95 | $2.1 |
| K | 16 | 0.94 | 1.00 | $2.8 |

So an RB bid labelled "80% to win" won two times in three, and K/DEF bids are higher than they need to be.

## The change
1. `warroom/build.py` `ladder(cases, estimates, positions)`: besides `lo`/`hi`, return `pos: {"RB": [...], ...}`, the sorted
   ratios (winning bid / raw estimate) per position. A position with fewer than 12 claims gets no ladder of its own and falls
   back to `lo`/`hi` (the same "at least 12 or merge" rule `hi` uses today). `main()` passes `data["P"]` positions, the
   way `shq/health.py` `warroom_prices` already does.
2. `warroom/model.js` `winChance(b, est, pos)`: use `D.ladder.pos[pos]` when it exists, else today's size split. Callers pass
   `D.P[sid].p`: `priceFor`, `bidOptions` (line 137), the best-bid search (line 209) and `win_curve` (line 253).
3. `shq/health.py` `win_chance` / `price_for` / `ladder_backtest` mirror `model.js`; change them in the same commit so the
   health check scores the ladder the page actually uses (it already imports `warroom.build.ladder`).
4. No position × size split: RB would drop to about 15 claims per side. Revisit when the ladder passes 300 claims.

What changes on screen: bid options and win chances on `/war-room-v2/` and in `bids.json` (`win_curve`, the 25/50/80% prices).
Expect RB prices up and K/DEF prices down; `price_ladder_claims` in the summary also lists claims per position.

## Test before shipping (out of sample)
Run `shq/health.py`'s leave-one-run-out backtest (`ladder_backtest`) with the new ladder on the same 165 claims (7 runs).
Ship only if all of these hold:
- RB at the 80% price: 0.67 → at least 0.75; at the 50% price: 0.37 → 0.45-0.60.
- K at the 50% price: 0.94 → 0.80 or less (stop overpaying); DEF at 80%: 0.95 → 0.90 or less.
- Overall `wr.ladder_cov50` stays within 0.40-0.60 and `wr.ladder_cov80` within 0.72-0.88 (their tolerances).
- No position moves further from its target than it is today by more than 0.10.
Also print the average 80% price per position before and after on the same claims, so the $ shift is visible.
After shipping, `wr.ladder_cov50`, `wr.ladder_cov80` and the `by` splits in the nightly health job are the measure.

## Risks and rollback
- Thin splits overfit: K has 16 claims and fewer when one run is held out. The 12-claim floor and the fallback cover it;
  watch K/DEF over the next 3 runs.
- Positions change value through the season (RB injuries); a later step could weight recent runs more (legs 4-7 already
  look better: 86% vs 77% at the 80% price).
- Rollback: revert the commit. `model.js` keeps working with an old `D.ladder` that has no `pos` key, so a stale page or
  bids.json is harmless.

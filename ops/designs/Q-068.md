# Q-068 · Judge the moved-players miss on pooled weeks and on the players actually averaged

Drafted by the nightly improvement pass, 2026-11-18. Nothing here is built until the item's Decision says approved.

## Problem
`moved.mae_gap` (hurt, traded and new-starter players' average projection miss minus everyone else's) went on watch on
Nov 18: +0.4 pts in week 10 vs a baseline of −0.4 (week 9, set Nov 12), tolerance 1.5. Three things make that flag weak
evidence:
- One game moves it. Week 10's group average (4.9) is over `mae_n` 52 players. George Pickens (tagged Q, projected
  13.5, scored 43.5) alone adds about 0.58; without him the group averages 4.41, under everyone else's 4.5.
- The row says n=134 (`moved_checks` passes the group size), but 82 of those were projected 0 and did not play,
  and are left out of the average (`moved_week`'s `err`). The MIN_N of 10 is checked against the wrong count.
- The baseline is a single week's gap, so the check compares one noisy week with another. Weeks 9-10 pooled give
  4.35 (n=96) vs 4.29 (n=533): +0.06.
`moved.zeroed` and `moved.inactive` (the checks that catch the Q-043 failures) were 0 both weeks and stay as they are.

## The change (`shq/health.py` only)
1. `moved_week`: also store `abs_sum` and `others_abs_sum` (the sums behind `mae` and `others_mae`), so weeks can be
   pooled from last night's stored details without refetching. Bump `MOVED_V` to 2 so weeks 9-10 are recomputed once
   with the new fields.
2. `moved_checks`: `moved.mae`, `moved.others_mae` and `moved.mae_gap` use the last 3 scored weeks pooled
   (sum of misses / players averaged), and their n is the players averaged (`mae_n` summed), not the group size.
   `moved.zeroed` / `moved.inactive` keep the latest week and the group n.
3. Make the gap a floor check instead of a baseline check: `CHECKS["moved.mae_gap"]` judged against 0 with a tolerance
   of 1.0 (watch over +0.5, drift over +1.0 pooled) once n ≥ 40 averaged players, so "their miss is more than everyone
   else's" is the question, not "more than the one week we happened to set as baseline". Remove its stored baseline.
4. The note adds the pooled line ("Weeks 9-10 pooled: 4.35 for them (n=96) vs 4.29 (n=533)") and the single biggest
   miss in the latest week, so a one-game spike is visible at a glance.
No page, push or bids.json change; the scorecard (Q-013, when built) reads the same rows.

## Test before shipping
- Unit tests in `tests/test_health_moved.py` (fixtures only): a week with one 30-pt miss in a 50-player group does not
  flag when the pooled gap is under +0.5; n on the gap row equals the players averaged; a stored v1 week is recomputed.
- Replay on this season: run `moved_health` from scratch for weeks 9-10 (git history of `players_nfl.json` and
  `state/ir_seen.json`, as the job does). Expected: pooled gap about +0.06 → `moved.mae_gap` ok; `moved.zeroed` 0 and
  `moved.inactive` 0 unchanged. If the pooled gap comes out above +0.5, the flag is real and this item turns into a
  projection fix instead (look at the Q-tagged under-projections first: Pickens, Mahomes, Samuel, Pollard all ran
  10+ pts over).
- After shipping: the Nov 24 run (week 11) must show `moved.mae_gap` ok or watch only if the pooled gap is over +0.5.

## Risks and rollback
- Pooling hides a sudden one-week break (e.g. a new trade deadline bug). The zeroed/inactive checks still judge the
  latest week alone, and those are the ones that catch zeroed or stale projections.
- The baseline file loses the `moved.mae_gap` entry; the change is in `shq/health.py`, not in
  `state/health_baselines.json` by hand (the job rewrites it).
- Rollback: `git revert` the commit; v1 weeks recompute on the next run.

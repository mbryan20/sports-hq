# Q-009 · The 0.3-point win-odds noise floor is a guess

Drafted by the nightly improvement pass, 2026-11-08. Nothing here is built until the item's Decision says approved.

## Problem
`warroom/model.js:23` `MIN_WIN = 0.003`: a move must add 0.3 points of win odds to be "Add free" or a bid
(`decideAll`, lines 206-211); below that it is "Skip: adds too little to matter". The comment says the simulation can't
tell smaller gains from noise, but that noise was never measured.

Tonight's bids.json (Nov 7 17:43Z) shows why it matters now: my win odds are 5%, so every gain is small, and the calls sit
on the floor: Pat Freiermuth and Tyler Higbee "Add free" at +0.003 (exactly the floor as stored, rounded to 3 decimals),
Dominic Zvada "Skip" at +0.002, Trey Smack, Cam Little and Wan'Dale Robinson "Skip" at +0.001. 9 of 14 rows are free adds,
so the floor decides most of what the page tells me to do this week.

How noisy is a gain? `simulate()` re-seeds with the same seed (`rng(20261027)`, line 74) on every call, so the base and
the with-move runs share random numbers, which cancels most noise, but not all: a changed lineup changes who is cut
each season, and the rest of the sequence shifts. The comparison uses `S.sims / 2` seasons: 3,000 on the page, 10,000
in bids.json, so the page's noise is about 1.8× bids.json's and the two can disagree near the floor (Q-006).
Not confirmed: the actual size (Node isn't installed on the laptop, so it wasn't measured tonight).

## The change
1. Measure: a script (`warroom/noise.js`, test-only) runs `gains()` for every candidate with 20 different seeds
   (`simulate` takes an optional seed; default unchanged), at 3,000 and at 10,000 seasons, on the 19 committed War Room
   snapshots (the `__DATA__` blob in each version of `docs/war-room-v2/index.html`). Output: each row's mean and standard
   deviation of `dWin`.
2. Set the floor from it: `MIN_WIN` = 2 × the median standard deviation at 10,000 seasons (bids.json), rounded up to
   0.001. If that is below 0.003, lower it; if above, raise it.
3. Page vs push: on the page, a row within one standard deviation of the floor gets a "≈ noise" tag instead of a
   different call from bids.json; Q-006 (show bids.json's own results at default settings) removes the gap for good.
4. Keep the fixed seed, so a rebuild with unchanged data gives the same calls.

## Test before shipping (out of sample)
- Pick the floor on the snapshots from weeks 1-7, then check weeks 8-9: the share of rows whose call flips between
  the 20 seeds must be under 5% at 10,000 seasons (today's share is the number to beat; record it here).
- Same seeds, same data: bids.json is byte-identical before and after when the floor doesn't change.
- No health.json check covers it (one-off: flip rate, then the floor is fixed). Later, Q-015's push log can show
  whether free adds flagged near the floor scored anything.

## Risks and rollback
- A higher floor hides small free adds (K/DEF streams); a lower one recommends noise. The test picks it from data.
- Only free adds and the "too small" gate change; bid sizes and prices don't.
- Rollback: put `MIN_WIN` back to 0.003.

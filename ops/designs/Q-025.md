# Q-025 · War Room v2's budget step drops a claim instead of fitting it

Drafted by the nightly improvement pass, 2026-11-10. Nothing here is built until the item's Decision says approved.

## Problem
`warroom/model.js` `decideAll` (lines 203-235) prices each claim alone: the bid with the best
(chance of winning) × (value − bid). Then lines 222-233 hand out money in order of that payoff, and line 231 sets a
claim to "Skip: your other bids use the budget" as soon as its own best bid no longer fits. It never tries a smaller
bid for that claim, or a dollar less on the ones before it.

Week 8 (the only waiver run with real money since bids.json started): spendable $54. Puka Nacua $14 (42% to win),
Javonte Williams $15 (40%), Jonathan Taylor $15 (40%) took $44; Parker Washington (value $31, went for $21) was
skipped outright; then George Pickens $2 and AJ Barner $1 still got money. All three coin flips would have lost
(winners paid $57, $34, $27). health.json: `rec.would_win` 0.2 (1 of 5), `bargains_skipped` 1.
What this design does not fix: the three coin flips lost because the price estimates were low ($27, $33, $33), which
is Q-003 and Q-004. A $10 bid would not have won Washington either. This is a logic fix with a small expected gain.

## The change
1. `decideAll`: keep the first pass (each claim's value and its payoff at every bid from $0 to its cap; `price()`
   already loops over them) and the drop rule (a later claim moves to its next-best drop and is re-valued).
2. Replace the greedy money loop with one small search over the claims that have a value: choose one bid per claim
   (including $0) so the bids add up to at most `spend` and the summed payoff is highest. Spend is at most $200 and
   there are at most about 10 priced claims, so a table over dollars (a knapsack with one option group per claim)
   is instant, in the browser too.
3. Calls after the search: a claim left at $0 → "Skip: your other bids use the budget" (as today); a claim given
   less than its stand-alone best → same call rule on its new win chance, `why: "trimmed to fit the budget"`.
4. `bids.json`: each row gains `bid_alone` (the stand-alone best bid) next to `bid`; `budget.recommended_bids_total`
   unchanged in meaning. The page's bid-options table marks the chosen bid as today.
The limit stays worst case (every bid wins), as now; the reserve is untouched.

## Test before shipping (out of sample)
Only one real-money run is on record and my FAAB is $10 ($5 spendable), so this season can't prove it. Use the league's
own history instead:
- Replay harness (`warroom/export.js --replay`, runs in GitHub Actions; Node isn't on the laptop): `model.py`
  `calibration_cases` already rebuilds the league as it stood at each past waiver run (rosters, FAAB, projections). For
  each of the 8 runs, build the ladder without that run (as the health job does for `wr.ladder_cov50`), run
  `decideAll` both ways for my team, and score each claim against the real winning bid: won if my bid beat it,
  surplus = value − bid when won.
- Ship if, over the 8 runs: total surplus is at least the greedy version's; no run is worse by more than $5; and no
  claim with value above its 50% price is left at $0 while a lower-payoff claim gets money.
- Unit tests in `tests/test_warroom.py`: bids never exceed `spendable` (exists, line 181); the week 8 case (four claims,
  $54) keeps all four in play; with one claim the result equals today's.
- health.json afterwards: `bargains_skipped` with the budget reason stays at 0; `rec.would_win` and `rec.win_brier`
  are watched, not gated (n is too small this season).

## Risks and rollback
- It moves money between claims every week it binds (Med). The replay shows by how much before shipping.
- The search maximises the model's own payoff, so wrong price estimates still mislead it (Q-003, Q-004).
- Rollback: revert the one commit to `model.js`; bids.json's extra field is ignored by its readers.

# Pool method

Copied from Michael's Google Drive › Football Pools › "Pool method (from ChatGPT project)" (Oct 22, 2026). The weekly runs follow this, plus the project rules in `RUNBOOK.md`.

1. **Fresh weekly evaluation.** Treat each week's slate as a new analysis. Do not carry forward prior-week team-specific opinions, picks, rankings, injury assumptions, market lines, score projections, or matchup conclusions. Prior context may be used only for pool rules, evaluation methodology, strategy, and process lessons. Rebuild all current-week conclusions from fresh research and current sources. Prior games and season-to-date performance may be used as evidence, but prior conclusions about those teams are not evidence.

2. **Symmetric straight-up pick validation.** For every matchup, independently evaluate both teams' outright win probabilities rather than defaulting to the market favorite. When the favorite's market-implied win probability is below ~65%, require affirmative matchup support and compare multiple independent probability estimates before finalizing. Record meaningful model disagreements, investigate whether either model overlooks injuries or matchup factors, and distinguish narrow favorites from high-confidence selections. Keep the higher threshold for outright underdogs. Never target a predetermined number of upsets or favorites. Use pool pick distributions only after establishing win probabilities; consider standings-based leverage separately from the most-likely-winner analysis.

3. **Market disagreement threshold.** Picking an outright underdog over the moneyline favorite requires multiple independent signals supporting the upset. Evidence must support the underdog's outright win probability; ATS models, cover probabilities, public-pick leverage, one favorable model, or one matchup angle are not sufficient alone. Sources relying on the same underlying model, stat, or argument count as one signal. Differentiation comes from well-supported disagreement with the market, not a lower evidence bar for unpopular picks.

4. **Matchup validation.** A team weakness is not actionable until the opponent is confirmed equipped to exploit it. Evaluate offense-vs-defense interaction, personnel, scheme, and efficiency splits, not isolated raw stats.

5. **Market movement audit.** For close straight-up decisions, compare opening/lookahead lines with the current spread and moneyline. Movement of ~2+ points or through a key number triggers investigation (injuries, QB news, matchup info, sharp reassessment) before finalizing. Movement is evidence to investigate, not an automatic reason to follow.

6. **Tiebreaker market anchor.** For exact-score projections, start with spread- and total-implied scores; keep matchup adjustments within ~2–3 points per team unless strong evidence (confirmed QB/injury news, pace, weather, extreme matchup) warrants more.

7. **Extreme-score tiebreakers.** For MOST and FEWEST points, use market-implied team totals as the baseline but evaluate each team's probability of producing the slate's extreme score, not just the highest/lowest mean. When close, weigh pace, offensive volatility, explosive-play potential, floor/ceiling, opponent scoring suppression, and blowout dynamics.

8. **Fake-money pool strategy.** Analyze separately from straight-up pick'em. Rank wagers by estimated fair line vs. pool line; separate handicapping edge from bankroll sizing. A small stale-line edge alone doesn't justify major concentration. Size by edge strength, standings deficit, weeks remaining, minimum-wager requirements, and downside risk. When free line adjustment is allowed with no payout penalty, use the full adjustment and prioritize crossing key numbers or turning a strong favorite into a near-moneyline proposition.

9. **Continuous improvement.** After results, grade the decision process separately from outcomes. Identify repeatable lessons (research, market interpretation, advanced stats, injuries, public-pick leverage, tiebreakers, thresholds). Recommend guideline changes only when generalizable. No permanent rules from isolated upsets or team-specific conclusions. Periodically consolidate to avoid contradictory or reactive rules.

## Project rules (from the Pool Picks setup)

- Fresh research every week: lines, injuries, QB news, weather, efficiency, personnel, scheme.
- Prior opinions aren't evidence. Underdogs need multiple independent signals. Win probability first, public leverage second.
- Every pick gets a reason of 12 words or fewer plus a source link; tiebreakers get one alternate option.
- College ratings come from stats only (EPA/play, success rate, opponent-adjusted scoring, prior-year SP+ scaled by returning production, QB/injury news, home field, weather). The spread is never a model input; it is shown alongside for comparison and blended only when simulating outcomes for pool strategy.
- Yahoo: an underdog only when the spread is about 4 or less, the stats model favors the dog, at least 2 independent signals agree, and the group is likely on the favorite; a 14-person pool sim decides how many such dogs to take.
- BTO and CFP: wager sizing is simulated under each pool's real rules to maximize the chance of finishing first (BTO: making the top-12 playoff with a high seed), not just expected value.
